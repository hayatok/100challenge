## Experience and art direction

Make the worker feel physically present in a bright coastal town: readable foot support, a clear change of silhouette when attacking, a purposeful reach when recovering cargo and a quieter stance after loss. Terrain should feel like limestone terraces, timber crossings and turquoise sea, with architecture framing actual routes. The tactical choices must remain readable during all effects.

Preserve the four adult women and their established identities. Isana is a warm, confident adult in her late twenties, with chestnut ponytail, golden-brown eyes, ochre raincoat, teal patterned scarf, charcoal cargo trousers, brown gloves/boots and a leather shoulder bag. Ritsu, Kohaku and Sui keep their distinct hair, palettes, occupations and silhouettes from the v0.3 portrait family. Use compact stylized adult proportions, broad clear shapes and painted surfaces; no childlike facial age or realistic skin pores. Retain the existing portraits until an actually better coherent replacement is demonstrated.

The initial 3D hypothesis was an approximately four-head-tall Isana with a recognizable face, separated limbs and a neutral T-pose suitable for rigging. The generated references and source model instead have approximately six-head adult proportions. This is a visible deviation to assess at the game camera before choosing the family. The short open raincoat and split tails must allow the legs to move. Keep gloves, boots, scarf and bag distinct; no tool fused into a hand. A held salvage tool is a separate attachment. Reference image: one complete character, front view, true transparent background, neutral even lighting, no floor/base, no text or extra views.

World palette: sunlit warm limestone, muted terracotta, deep teal shaded wall faces, turquoise water and restrained flowering greenery. Use coherent directional sunlight, soft ambient fill and visible contact shadows. No unbounded bloom, decorative particles over the next action or uniform plaza dressing. Danger retains outlined coral directions/symbols; loot gold and active selection cool teal/ivory stay distinct from scenery.

HUD typography and primary controls remain the v0.4 instrument system. Harbor and results need scenes with a deliberate composition, clear large headings, actual cargo/mission progress and a prominent next action. Replace sprawling repeated paper panels where they weaken hierarchy. Long dialogue/quotes must not leave closing punctuation isolated. Noto Sans CJK JP remains licensed and legible; animation never delays access to the real inventory/result.

On phones, the harbor should showcase the selected worker and current voyage, with four roster portraits, concise actual mission progress, services and an always-visible basic-loadout shortcut. Put detailed carry/stash choices in a dedicated preparation pane opened explicitly; avoid leaving only one inventory row visible below a tall permanent header. The preparation pane must show real equipped/owned items, loan/capacity restrictions, exact UID transfers and a close action. Preserve its open state, scroll and keyboard focus after a transfer; pause the background stage while reading it. Desktop may place preparation alongside the character scene. Results use a scene/character area and a distinct actual cargo receipt/grid, with readable full-width dialogue and an immediate pinned next action. A monolithic pale form alone does not meet the visual goal.

## Bounded method comparison

Two generated walk atlases failed the gait gate: most cells repeated the same leading foot. A modular 2D front atlas then demonstrated independent joints, support/passing phases and action channels. Its first source had open knee cavities and was rejected. One imagegen correction removed those; the corrected native prototype has 21 pivots and dedicated attack/hurt/search. This is front-only feasibility. Its near-front perspective, unified silhouette and especially real-speed cadence remain unresolved: the probe walks one tile over 3.2 seconds, whereas copying its four-footfall gait into the existing 0.22-second move would be too fast. The production family has not been adopted.

Compare one rigged 3D Isana before generating the other workers. The user authorized the Tripo browser subscription route and explicitly switched this task to Chrome after the in-app login failed. The user also explicitly authorized multiview and higher-credit quality features for this model. Use the logged-in Studio UI, not a paid API/CLI replacement. First pilot: consistent front/back/left/right references, one high-quality multiview model generation, then suitable topology/texturing, its rig and representative idle/walk/attack/hurt clips if the visible subscription controls support them. Prefer quality over the cheapest setting; inspect each completed stage before expanding the family. Record displayed operation costs and actual completed outputs; no subscription change or top-up. Preserve the GPT references, original GLBs and provenance. A failed pilot is a result to evaluate, not a reason to label a placeholder complete.

The 3D comparison uses one shared SubViewport/World3D for terrain, actor, water, light and shadow, inside a Control facade. Map logical (x,y) to 3D (x,height,z) without changing Rules. Preserve `present`, visible-cell queries, playable bounds, preview data, pause and `cell_selected`. Pick the actual height-aware cell surface; an intersection with only the y=0 plane would misalign raised-cell taps. Use Rules visibility/threat data, including seen-but-not-visible fog; prevent hidden buildings/enemies/shadows from leaking through it. Landmarks follow real saved anchors. Legacy maps retain their actual dimensions and their existing presentation fallback during the pilot.

## Gates and production sequence

1. Inspect the one-character imported rig: actual skeleton, mesh/materials, orientation/feet, clip names/durations, all four facings, gait contact/passing, attack/recoil/search silhouettes. Compare at actual phone scale beside the v0.4 actor. A static model or a whole-mesh bob does not pass.
2. Render one actual v0.4 encounter with that actor, raised/low ground, sea, occlusion, cargo and shared enemy intent. A labelled blockout can test camera/geometry; it cannot count as finished environment art. Keep all pilot code/assets in ignored scratch until a method is selected.
3. Operate native and Web at 390x844, 360x740 and desktop. Check action-to-result timing, one deliberate input per turn, rapid-input convergence, the two-cell technique, attacks/hurt ordering, reading during animation, tap alignment, focus/modal pause and reduced motion. Measure frame time and initial asset load without lowering quality merely by assumption. Do not infer human fun from automation.
4. Choose the method from the comparison, then create a coherent family for the other three women and the needed district architecture/props. Record originals, prompts, subscription task/download evidence, normalization/import and actual use. Generate only what the adopted scene needs.
5. Integrate harbor/conversation/result staging with that family and current mission state. Results must immediately list real items/count/value and the next action; no ownership change from a clip callback. Preserve a static reduced-motion outcome and focus/modal freeze.
6. Run appropriate regressions/export, actual operation and screen comparisons, Japanese run/verify and mandatory Astra review. Update the draftPR219 with the final adopted scope and current-head CI. Only then merge and publish v0.4+v0.5 through the existing Pages route and verify the same-run Linux artifact/public game.

Physical devices, Safari, listening, endurance, human enjoyment and commercial acceptance remain separate unverified gates. Further versions should address observed weaknesses, including repeated authored encounters and quiet cleared return routes, rather than inflating a feature count.
